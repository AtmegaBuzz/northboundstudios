## Section-by-section layout

### 1. Hero
**Purpose:** One clear statement of what you do. Nothing else.
**Visual:** Oversized type as the visual element — headline dominates the viewport. Optional: single documentary photo or subtle motion behind it.
**Copy budget:** Headline ≤10 words. Subline ≤20 words. One CTA button.

Headline options:
- *"We find what's slowing your business down — then build the fix."*
- *"Systems that bring you customers."*
- *"We build the systems that grow your business."*

### 2. Credibility strip
**Purpose:** Instant proof, no reading required.
**Visual:** Thin horizontal band. Client logos if you have them; large numbers if you don't (years shipping, systems built, platforms worked across).
**Copy budget:** 3–4 items, 2–4 words each.

### 3. The problem
**Purpose:** Reader recognizes themselves.
**Visual:** Three equal blocks, numbered 01/02/03. Icon or number is the visual anchor, not an illustration.
**Copy budget:** Per block — 3-word label + one sentence.

Suggested three:
- **Invisible** — customers can't find you in search or AI answers.
- **Manual** — your team runs on spreadsheets, WhatsApp threads and copy-paste.
- **Leaking** — leads arrive and go cold before anyone follows up.

### 4. What we do (the three pillars)
**Purpose:** Show range without a wall of text.
**Visual:** Three columns or a 3-row stack. Each pillar = number + title + sub-service list set small. Lists do the work; no paragraphs.
**Copy budget:** Per pillar — title + one sentence + 5–6 sub-items.

### 5. How we work
**Purpose:** De-risk the engagement. Show it's a process, not a black box.
**Visual:** Horizontal 4-step sequence with thin rule lines between steps. Numbers large.
**Copy budget:** Per step — one word + one line.

1. **Audit** — we map how your business actually runs and where it leaks.
2. **Plan** — you get a prioritized list of fixes with expected impact.
3. **Build** — we implement, scoped from the audit.
4. **Support** — ongoing maintenance and expansion.

### 6. Work / proof
**Purpose:** Evidence. This is the section that converts.
**Visual:** One large case study, image-led, not a grid of thumbnails.
**Until you have a client:** replace with a live demo (a working tool visitors can try) or a "what we're building now" block. Don't ship an empty portfolio grid — an empty section costs more trust than no section.
**Copy budget:** Title + 2 sentences + one metric.

### 7. Who we work with
**Purpose:** Broad enough to not exclude anyone, specific enough to feel real.
**Visual:** Simple list or small tiles. No icons needed.
**Copy budget:** 4–6 business types, 2–3 words each.

### 8. CTA
**Purpose:** One action.
**Visual:** Full-width color block. Background flips to your accent or near-black.
**Copy budget:** Headline ≤8 words + one line + button.

- *"Find out what's costing you customers."*
- Button: **Book a free audit**

### 9. Footer
Minimal. Logo, contact, one email, social links. Nothing else.

## Layout rules for a visual-heavy page

- **Type is the visual.** In an editorial system, scale and whitespace carry the design. Oversized headlines do more than stock illustration.
- **One idea per screen.** Each section should fill roughly one viewport and hold a single message.
- **No paragraphs over 3 sentences** anywhere on the page.
- **Whitespace between sections** should be generous — 120–160px desktop, 80px mobile.
- **Photography, if used:** real and documentary. Consistent crop ratios. No stock smiling-people-at-laptops.
- **Total page copy:** aim for under 400 words across the whole page.

## Open item

Your description here — "we help identify business problems and fix them, thus scaling your business" — is industry-agnostic, while earlier we'd settled on travel and tourism as the positioning. Both are viable, but they produce different hero copy and a different "who we work with" section. Worth deciding before the page is built:

- **Travel/tourism positioned:** narrower, converts better with that audience, harder to expand later.
- **Industry-agnostic growth studio:** wider net, less memorable, competes with far more agencies.

A middle path: industry-agnostic hero, with travel/tourism named first in "who we work with" and in your case studies. Keeps the door open while showing where you have depth.
